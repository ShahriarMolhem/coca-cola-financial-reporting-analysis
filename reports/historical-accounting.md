## Cash-versus-earnings observation

In 2025, **consolidated net income was $13.137 billion** and **operating cash flow was $7.408 billion**; the difference **was $5.729 billion**. Do **not** characterize this discrepancy by itself as abnormal accrual accounting or manipulation. The cash flow statement explicitly lists a **$7.208 billion net cash outflow associated with changes in operating assets and liabilities**, and Note 21 discusses the underlying transactions, including the **fairlife contingent consideration payment**. In 2024, the statement reports a $6.234 billion outflow in operating assets/liabilities and management describes the **IRS tax-litigation deposit**. These are critical context for interpreting operating cash conversion.

The largest selected FY2025 profit margin improvement from FY2024 reflects the sharp fall in **other operating charges** (2024: $4.163bn; 2025: $1.261bn), not merely an improvement in sales.

## Audit-trail conventions

The indirect method reconciles **consolidated net income** (includes income attributable to noncontrolling interests), rather than the net-income-attributable-to-shareowners headline. CFO/earnings ratio is illustrative and should not be called an audited earnings-quality conclusion. Simple free cash flow = reported CFO less cash purchases of PP&E; it is **not unlevered FCFF**.

The 2024 and 2025 balance-sheet current assets include held-for-sale classifications that can make comparisons less straightforward.

This is undergraduate historical statement analysis, **not** an audit or CPA opinion.
