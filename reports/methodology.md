# Methodology — From Income Statement to Cash Flow

**Objective:** A source-verified, reproducible, undergraduate accounting analysis, without pretending to perform a CPA audit.

## 1. Extract actual GAAP data
Record FY2021–FY2025 comparative Income Statements and Statements of Cash Flows; FY2022–FY2025 year-end comparative Balance Sheets; selected OCI categories. Use USD **millions**. Document original section names and sources.

## 2. Reconciliation controls
- **Gross profit check:** Net operating revenues minus COGS equals gross profit.
- **Operating income check:** Gross profit less SG&A and other operating charges equals operating income.
- **Net income check:** Pretax income minus taxes equals consolidated net income; consolidated NI less NCI NI equals shareowners' NI.
- **Balance sheet check:** Total assets less total liabilities less total equity equals zero.
- **Indirect CFO check:** Consolidated net income plus all company-reported cash flow adjustments (including operating asset/liability changes) equals CFO.
- Flag if checks disagree; do **not** force an unexplained plug.

## 3. Accounting analytics
Calculate common-size revenue/COGS/SG&A, gross and operating margins, effective income-tax rate, accrual difference `Consolidated Net Income – CFO`, CFO/net income, illustrative simple FCF, balance sheet current ratio and working capital. Track OCI and FX translation versus consolidated net income.

## 4. Investigate cash-conversion context
Reconcile published CFO using company statement adjustments. Discuss the impact of the **FY2025 fairlife contingent consideration payout** and the **FY2024 IRS tax litigation deposit**, with Note 21 and the MD&A disclosures. Do not claim every difference between NI and CFO is accounting quality deterioration.

## 5. Reporting
Build a formula-based Excel workbook with source tabs and checks; produce a 10-slide explanatory presentation and reusable speech transcript. Avoid unverified management forecasts and accounting-fraud claims.

## Limits
Financial-statement comparisons, not a full audit, policy compliance opinion, revenue-recognition test or internal-control evaluation. All historical facts come from public information, no access to Coca-Cola internal ledgers.
