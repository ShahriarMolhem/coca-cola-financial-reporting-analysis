# Coca-Cola | Accounting & Financial Reporting Analysis

**Shahriar Molhem** · BBA Finance, University of Georgia — Terry College of Business  
**Skills practiced:** GAAP statements, accrual accounting, operating cash flow, liquidity, other comprehensive income (OCI)

*Company: The Coca-Cola Company (NYSE: KO), not Coca-Cola Consolidated (NASDAQ: COKE).*

## The question behind the project

How can a company show strong profits but report much less cash flow from operations?

I reviewed Coca-Cola's public financial statements for **FY2021–FY2025** to connect the income statement with the cash flow statement, balance sheet, and OCI. My aim was to explain the financial reporting relationships, not make a judgment about the company from one ratio.

## Findings worth discussing

- **Profitability improved in 2025.** Revenue reached **$47.94 billion**, and operating income rose to **$13.76 billion**. The drop in other operating charges from **$4.16 billion** (2024) to **$1.26 billion** (2025) is important context.
- **Earnings and operating cash were different.** FY2025 consolidated net income was **$13.14 billion**, versus **$7.41 billion** in operating cash flow—a difference of **$5.73 billion**.
- **The footnotes matter.** Operating assets and liabilities contributed a **$7.21 billion** negative adjustment in the 2025 cash flow reconciliation. The *fairlife* contingent consideration payment and the prior-year IRS tax-litigation deposit make simple year-over-year comparisons less useful.
- **Balance sheet and OCI tell another part of the story.** The reported current ratio rose from **1.03×** in 2024 to **1.46×** in 2025. Foreign-currency translation OCI swung from **−$2.89 billion** to **+$2.87 billion**.

These observations **do not** suggest that a lower cash-flow-to-earnings ratio is proof of accounting misconduct. They demonstrate why statement definitions and related notes are essential.

## What I learned

The indirect method gave me a better way to explain why net income and cash flow differ. It also reinforced the importance of checking classifications before comparing liquidity ratios or equity movements across years. I used formula-linked Excel tables and tested the main accounting identities; that is not the same as performing an audit.
