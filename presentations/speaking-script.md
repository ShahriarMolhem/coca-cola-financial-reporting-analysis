# Coca-Cola Financial Reporting — Slide-by-Slide Presentation Script

**Presenter:** Shahriar Molhem · University of Georgia · undergraduate accounting/finance portfolio  
**Target length:** 8–10 minutes. All figures are historical GAAP results from Coca-Cola's public SEC filings.

## Slide 1 — Introduction (about 40 seconds)
Hi, I'm Shahriar Molhem, a finance student at the University of Georgia. For this portfolio project, I focused on the accounting side of The Coca-Cola Company's publicly reported financial statements over five years, from 2021 through 2025. The goal is to connect three areas: profitability, the difference between earnings and cash flow, and how the balance sheet and comprehensive income add context. This is an independent educational analysis, not an audit.

## Slide 2 — The central question (about 45 seconds)
Financial statements do more than report profit. They tell different parts of the same business story. The income statement measures performance under accrual accounting. The cash flow statement traces operating cash. The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time. I organized the project around one question: when those statements show different trends, how can I reconcile the numbers and explain what they mean?

## Slide 3 — Five-year performance (about 55 seconds)
Coca-Cola's reported operating revenue increased from approximately 38.7 billion dollars in 2021 to 47.9 billion dollars in 2025. Operating income also increased overall, from roughly 10.3 to 13.8 billion dollars. But the path wasn't smooth. The operating margin was about 26.7 percent in 2021, declined to roughly 21.2 percent in 2024 and then increased to approximately 28.7 percent in 2025. That tells me to investigate year-specific expense lines instead of simply assuming every year was consistently better.

## Slide 4 — Understanding operating charges (about 55 seconds)
In 2025, operating revenue grew modestly, about 1.9 percent compared with 2024, but reported operating income increased significantly. A key accounting detail is that other operating charges decreased from approximately 4.16 billion dollars in 2024 to 1.26 billion dollars in 2025. This helps explain the difference in reported margins. I do not automatically remove these charges or call all of them one-time expenses; to make that judgment, I would need to review the relevant notes.

## Slide 5 — Earnings versus operating cash (about 55 seconds)
This is the central part of my project. In 2025, Coca-Cola reported about 13.14 billion dollars in consolidated net income, but only about 7.41 billion in cash provided by operating activities. That's a gap of approximately 5.73 billion dollars. Accrual accounting and cash movements can create real differences between those figures. On its own, the ratio doesn't establish a problem with earnings quality. We need to understand the cash flow reconciliation and significant transactions.

## Slide 6 — Indirect method (about 65 seconds)
To explain the cash flow, I use the indirect method from the SEC filing. We start with consolidated net income, not just net income attributable to Coca-Cola shareowners. Then we adjust for noncash expenses and other items, including depreciation, stock compensation, deferred taxes, equity-method income and gains or losses. One especially important reported item in 2025 is a negative 7.21 billion dollars from changes in operating assets and liabilities. After all the adjustments, the result reconciles to reported operating cash flow of 7.41 billion dollars.

## Slide 7 — Financial statement context (about 55 seconds)
The financial statement notes are critical here. For 2025, the fairlife contingent consideration payment is an important part of the cash-flow story. For 2024, Coca-Cola reported a significant deposit related to IRS tax litigation. Both events affect how we interpret year-over-year operating cash flow. So I would not look at the cash conversion percentage alone and conclude that the company's regular business cash generation suddenly collapsed or that there was an accounting violation.

## Slide 8 — Balance sheet (about 55 seconds)
I also compared two year-end balance sheets. Reported current assets increased from about 26.0 billion dollars in 2024 to 31.0 billion in 2025, while current liabilities declined from about 25.2 to 21.3 billion. That increased the reported current ratio from approximately 1.03 to 1.46 times. However, the balance sheet also includes assets and liabilities classified as held for sale. That classification is an important limitation when discussing short-term liquidity.

## Slide 9 — Other comprehensive income (about 50 seconds)
Comprehensive income captures some changes in value that don't flow through current net income. Coca-Cola's foreign-currency translation adjustment moved from a negative 2.89 billion dollars in 2024 to a positive 2.87 billion dollars in 2025. This is a large swing that helps explain why movements in equity can differ from the net income number. It also shows why accounting analysis should go beyond the income statement.

## Slide 10 — Final takeaways (about 45 seconds)
To summarize, this project taught me three practical accounting lessons. First, reported profitability and operating cash flow are related but are not the same. Second, reading footnotes is necessary when unusual cash payments affect year-over-year comparisons. And third, cross-checking the income statement, cash flow statement, balance sheet and comprehensive income helps produce a more reliable analysis. I built the analysis in Excel using publicly reported figures, and I documented the methods and limitations. Thank you, and I'd be happy to explain my workbook and calculations.
